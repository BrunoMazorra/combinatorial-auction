\title{On false-name proof combinatorial auctions}

% Anonymized submission.
\author{Bruno Mazorra}

% Abstract. Note that this must come before \maketitle.
\begin{abstract}
Iwasaki et al. \cite{iwasaki2010worst} show that, under anonymity, symmetry across goods, and independence of irrelevant goods (IIG), the optimal worst-case welfare approximation ratio of deterministic false-name-proof combinatorial auctions with single-minded bidders is $(m+1)/2$, where $m$ is the number of goods. In this paper, we make two contributions. First, we show that this bound continues to hold without the IIG assumption. Second, we show that allowing randomization substantially improves worst-case welfare. Specifically, we construct a randomized mechanism that is false-name-proof in expectation, runs in polynomial time, and achieves a worst-case welfare approximation ratio of $O(\sqrt{m}\log m)$. Our mechanism incurs only an additional $O(\log m)$ factor in welfare approximation compared to the classical greedy algorithm, showing that randomization makes false-name-proofness compatible with computational efficiency.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

We consider combinatorial auctions with a single seller, a set
$M=[m]$ of indivisible goods, and a finite set $N$ of bidders,
where $m\ge1$ and $n=|N|$. Bidder $i$ has a private type
$\theta_i=(S_i,v_i)$, consisting of a nonempty target
$S_i\subseteq M$ and a value $v_i\ge0$. With a slight abuse of
notation, we also use $v_i$ for its single-minded valuation
function: $v_i(R)=v_i\mathbf{1}\{S_i\subseteq R\}$ for every
bundle $R\subseteq M$. We denote the type space by $\Theta_M$.
Bidders are risk-neutral and have quasi-linear utilities:
receiving $R$ and paying $P$ gives utility $v_i(R)-P$.


A direct mechanism $\mathcal M=(X,P)$ maps a report profile
$\hat\theta=(\hat\theta_i)_{i\in N}$, with
$\hat\theta_i=(\hat S_i,b_i)\in\Theta_M$, to allocated bundles
$X_i(\hat\theta)\subseteq M$ and payments $P_i(\hat\theta)$
to the seller. The mechanism is defined for every finite set of
participating identities, including the empty set. Allocations
and payments may be randomized; all reports and identities are
fixed before the randomness is realized. Throughout, we require
\emph{ex-post feasibility}: for every report profile,
$X_i(\hat\theta)\cap X_j(\hat\theta)=\varnothing$ almost surely
for distinct $i,j$. Goods may remain unallocated. When no
confusion can arise, we drop the hat on reported targets and
write a report as $(S_i,b_i)$.

Write $u_i(\theta_i;\hat\theta)
=v_i(X_i(\hat\theta))-P_i(\hat\theta)$ for realized utility, and
\[
x_i(\hat\theta)
=\Pr[\hat S_i\subseteq X_i(\hat\theta)],
\qquad
\pi_i(\hat\theta)=\mathbb{E}[P_i(\hat\theta)]
\]
for service probabilities and expected payments. At a truthful
profile $\theta$, bidder $i$'s expected utility is
$U_i(\theta)=\mathbb{E}[u_i(\theta_i;\theta)]
=v_i x_i(\theta)-\pi_i(\theta)$.

We require \emph{individual rationality} (IR) in expectation,
meaning $U_i(\theta)\ge0$ for every bidder and type profile, and
\emph{no positive transfers} (NPT), meaning
$P_i(\hat\theta)\ge0$ almost surely for every report profile.
The expectation in IR is only over the mechanism's randomness,
with all other reports fixed, not over other bidders' types.
Thus $0\le\pi_i(\hat\theta)\le b_i x_i(\hat\theta)$.
For deterministic mechanisms, this is ex-post IR. For randomized
mechanisms, \emph{ex-post IR} additionally requires truthful
realized utility to be nonnegative almost surely.

\subsection{Truthfulness and false-name-proofness}

Let $E$ denote an arbitrary finite profile of external reports,
and write $(\hat\theta_i,E)$ for the profile obtained by adding
identity $i$ with report $\hat\theta_i$. A mechanism is
\emph{truthful} if, for every true type $\theta_i$, alternative
report $\hat\theta_i$, and external profile $E$,
\[
U_i(\theta_i,E)
\ge
\mathbb{E}\bigl[u_i(\theta_i;(\hat\theta_i,E))\bigr].
\]
For deterministic mechanisms, this is dominant-strategy
incentive compatibility (DSIC); for randomized mechanisms, it is
truthfulness in expectation. A deviation may change both the
target bundle and its value. A randomized mechanism is
\emph{universally truthful} if truthful reporting maximizes
realized utility for every realization of its randomness, that
is, if it is a distribution over DSIC mechanisms
\citep{dobzinski2006truthful}.

\emph{False-name-proofness} (FNP) \citep{yokoo2004effect} allows a
physical bidder to
replace its truthful report with any finite family of reports
$\hat\theta_A=(\hat\theta_j)_{j\in A}$ under distinct identities
$A$, its \emph{aliases}, not appearing in
$E$. The bidder values the union of their allocations and pays
the sum of their payments. We require
\[
U_i(\theta_i,E)
\ge
\mathbb{E}\!\left[
  v_i\!\left(\bigcup_{j\in A}X_j(\hat\theta_A,E)\right)
\right]
-\sum_{j\in A}\pi_j(\hat\theta_A,E)
\]
for every true type, external profile, and finite report family.
For randomized mechanisms, this is \emph{FNP in expectation};
for deterministic mechanisms, the expectation disappears.
The family may include the original identity, and different
identities may submit identical reports. Creating identities
is costless, and there is no bound on their number. We allow
$A=\varnothing$, representing nonparticipation, with empty unions
and sums interpreted as $\varnothing$ and zero. All submitted
identities remain in the profile and all assessed payments must
be paid: we consider ordinary FNP, without withdrawal. Taking
$|A|=1$ recovers truthfulness.

\subsection{Anonymity, symmetry, and irrelevant goods}

A mechanism is \emph{anonymous in utilities} if relabelling
bidders leaves their truthful expected utilities unchanged.
For a bijection $\tau:N\to N'$, define
$(\tau\theta)_{\tau(i)}=\theta_i$. Anonymity requires
$U_{\tau(i)}(\tau\theta)=U_i(\theta)$ for every profile, bidder,
and bijection. This definition applies to deterministic and
randomized mechanisms alike, and permits identity-based
tie-breaking when it leaves utilities unchanged.

Separately, a mechanism is \emph{symmetric across goods} if
$U_i(\sigma\theta)=U_i(\theta)$ for every bidder, profile, and
permutation $\sigma$ of $M$. Here $\sigma\theta$ replaces each
type $(S_j,v_j)$ by $(\sigma(S_j),v_j)$, so utilities on the
left are evaluated using the relabelled targets.

\begin{lemma}[Utility symmetry and critical prices]
\label{lem:utility-symmetry}
For a deterministic truthful mechanism satisfying IR and NPT,
anonymity in utilities is equivalent to anonymity of critical
prices. Likewise, symmetry across goods in utilities is
equivalent to symmetry across goods of critical prices.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix an identity $i$, an external profile $E$, and a target $S$.
DSIC makes service monotone in the reported value and gives
all served reports the same payment
\citep{lehmann2002truth,mualem2008truthful}. IR and NPT imply that
unserved reports pay zero and that the winning payment equals
the critical price $c_{i,E}(S)$, the infimum of winning bids.
Consequently,
\[
U_i((S,v),E)=\max\{0,v-c_{i,E}(S)\}.
\]
If the critical price is infinite, this utility is identically
zero. At a finite critical price, winning and losing both give
zero utility, so tie-breaking does not affect the formula.
The critical price can therefore be recovered from utilities:
\[
c_{i,E}(S)
=\inf\{v\ge0:U_i((S,v),E)>0\},
\]
with $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$. Thus invariance of utilities
under either relabelling implies the corresponding invariance
of critical prices; the converse follows from the utility
formula. Under bidder anonymity, we may consequently write a
common menu $c_E(S)$ depending only on $S$ and the multiset
of external reports. Symmetry across goods further gives
$c_{\sigma E}(\sigma(S))=c_E(S)$.
\end{proof}

For comparison with the deterministic benchmark of
\cite[Definition~2]{iwasaki2010worst}, \emph{independence of
irrelevant goods} (IIG) requires that, for sufficiently large
values, a bidder winning the grand bundle continues to win the
enlarged grand bundle when a new good is added only to its
target, with all values and other targets unchanged.

\subsection{The cover inequality}
\label{sec:cover}

False-name-proofness admits a concise characterization in terms of
critical prices, which underpins both the deterministic upper
bound in Section~\ref{sec:deterministic} and the randomized
mechanism in Section~\ref{sec:randomized}. Let
$\mathcal M$ be universally truthful, ex-post IR, and NPT. For
every realization of its randomness, the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:utility-symmetry} applies: a report $(S,b)$ of
identity $i$ against an external profile $E$ is served if
$b>c_{i,E}(S)$, is not served if $b<c_{i,E}(S)$, and pays
$c_{i,E}(S)$ when served. The critical prices are now random
variables.

\begin{proposition}[Cover inequality]
\label{prop:cover}
Let $\mathcal M$ be universally truthful, ex-post IR, and NPT.
Fix a bidder $i$ of type $(S,v)$, an external profile $E$, and a
finite family $\hat\theta_A$ of alias reports $(S_j,b_j)$,
$j\in A$. Let $D\subseteq A$ be the random set of aliases that
are served.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cover-general}
\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\sum_{j\in D}
c_{j,E\cup\hat\theta_{A\setminus\{j\}}}(S_j)\Bigr]
\ge
\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbf{1}\Bigl\{S\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in A}X_j(\hat\theta_A,E)\Bigr\}\,
c_{i,E}(S)\Bigr],
\end{equation}
then the family $\hat\theta_A$ does not improve upon truthful
reporting. In particular, a mechanism that
satisfies~\eqref{eq:cover-general} for all such $i$, $S$, $E$,
and $\hat\theta_A$ is false-name-proof in expectation.
\item[(b)] If $\mathcal M$ is deterministic and
false-name-proof, then~\eqref{eq:cover-general} holds: whenever
$S\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in A}X_j(\hat\theta_A,E)$,
\[
c_{i,E}(S)\le\sum_{j\in D}
c_{j,E\cup\hat\theta_{A\setminus\{j\}}}(S_j).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Served aliases pay their critical prices and the other aliases
pay zero, so the realized utility of the family is $v$ times the
indicator in~\eqref{eq:cover-general}, minus the sum on its
left-hand side.

(a) By~\eqref{eq:cover-general}, the expected utility of the
family is at most
\[
\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\mathbf{1}\Bigl\{S\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in A}X_j(\hat\theta_A,E)\Bigr\}\,
(v-c_{i,E}(S))\Bigr]
\le
\mathbb{E}\bigl[\max\{0,v-c_{i,E}(S)\}\bigr]
=U_i(\theta_i,E).
\]

(b) Suppose that $S\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in A}X_j(\hat\theta_A,E)$
and that the sum is smaller than $c_{i,E}(S)$. A bidder whose
value $v$ lies strictly between the two has truthful utility zero,
whereas the family gives it $v$ minus the sum, which is positive.
\end{proof}

For deterministic mechanisms, false-name-proofness is thus
equivalent to the cover inequality: the price of a bundle is at
most the total price paid by aliases that jointly acquire it.
This is the single-minded form of the
no-super-additive-price-increase condition by which
\citet{yokoo2003characterization} characterizes false-name-proof
protocols; see also the sub-additivity condition of
\citet{todo2009characterizing}. Part~(a) states what
randomization buys: the inequality need only hold on average.
Section~\ref{sec:deterministic} shows that the pointwise
inequality forces a linear loss of welfare, and
Section~\ref{sec:randomized} constructs a mechanism that
satisfies it in expectation only.

\subsection{Worst-case welfare}

At a truthful profile $\theta$, realized welfare is
$\mathrm{SW}_{\mathcal M}(\theta)=\sum_{i\in N}v_i(X_i(\theta))$,
with expectation $\sum_{i\in N}v_i x_i(\theta)$, and optimal
welfare is
$\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)=\max_{A\text{ feasible}}\sum_{i\in N}v_i(A_i)$.
For a fixed number $m$ of goods, we define the
\emph{worst-case welfare fraction}\footnote{Its reciprocal is
the usual worst-case welfare approximation ratio, with
$1/0=+\infty$.} by the following infimum over all finite
populations and type profiles on $m$ goods, taking the
expectation over the mechanism's randomness at each fixed
profile:
\[
\rho(\mathcal M)
=
\inf_{\theta:\,\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)>0}
\frac{\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{SW}_{\mathcal M}(\theta)]}
     {\mathrm{OPT}(\theta)}.
\]
\section{Worst-case welfare of deterministic mechanisms}
\label{sec:deterministic}

Throughout this section, the mechanism is deterministic, feasible,
ex-post IR and NPT, DSIC, and ordinary FNP.  It is defined for
every finite population and admits a common bidder-anonymous critical-price
menu, equivariant under permutations of the goods
(Lemma~\ref{lem:utility-symmetry}); as there, we write $c_E(S)$ for this menu at
external profile $E$.  Thus bids strictly above $c_E(S)$ are served, bids
strictly below it are not served, and a winner pays $c_E(S)$; behavior
at equality is fixed by the tie rule.  We do not assume independence of
irrelevant goods.

If $\rho(\mathcal M)>0$, every critical price is finite.  Indeed, if
$c_E(S)=\infty$, then a bidder with target $S$ loses at every finite
value $v$.  The mechanism's welfare is at most $\OPT(E)$, whereas the new
optimum is at least $v$; sending $v\to\infty$ contradicts a positive ratio.
We use this observation without further comment.

Under bidder anonymity, the cover inequality of
Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}(b) states that for any external
profile $E$, true target $S$, and finite false-name family
$\hat\theta_A$ with winning aliases $D\subseteq A$, whenever
$S\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in A}X_j(\hat\theta_A,E)$ we have
$c_E(S)\le\sum_{j\in D}c_{E\cup\hat\theta_{A\setminus\{j\}}}(S_j)$,
with every losing alias remaining in the price context on the
right-hand side.

For $h>0$, write $H_h=(M,h)$ for the grand-bundle report of value $h$.
Goods equivariance gives a common clean singleton price
\[
  t(h)=c_{\{H_h\}}(\{g\}),
  \qquad g\in M.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Clean prices have a near-flat pair]
\label{lem:no-iig-flat}
For every $h>0$, $0<t(h)<\infty$.  Moreover, for every $\eps>0$ there exist
$h<h'$ in $[1,2]$ such that
\begin{equation}
  t(h')\le(1+\eps)t(h).
  \label{eq:near-flat}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Finiteness was established above.  If $t(h)=0$, then every singleton bid
$\delta>0$ strictly wins against $H_h$.  Feasibility forces the grand-bundle
bidder to lose, so the welfare ratio on this two-bidder profile is at most
$\delta/h$.  Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ contradicts positivity.

For the second claim, fix an integer $\ell\ge1$ and consider the grid
points $1+s/\ell$, $s=0,\ldots,\ell$.  If every adjacent pair satisfied
$t(1+(s+1)/\ell)>(1+\eps)\,t(1+s/\ell)$, then
\[
  t(2)>(1+\eps)^\ell\, t(1).
\]
Because both endpoint prices are positive and finite, this fails for all
sufficiently large $\ell$.  One adjacent pair therefore satisfies
\eqref{eq:near-flat}.
\end{proof}

The next lemma is the replacement for IIG.  A collection of singleton bids
below half the clean price can neither lower another singleton's price nor
co-win with a higher singleton.

\begin{lemma}[Half-price persistence]
\label{lem:no-iig-half}
Fix $h>0$ and a good $g$, write $t=t(h)$, and choose $\lambda<t/2$.  Let $L$
be any family of distinct singleton reports, all of value $\lambda$, on goods
other than $g$.  Then
\begin{equation}
  c_{\{H_h\}\cup L}(\{g\})\ge t.
  \label{eq:no-iig-half}
\end{equation}
Moreover, if $(\{g\},y)$ wins against $\{H_h\}\cup L$ for some $y>\lambda$, no
report in $L$ co-wins.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We induct on $|L|$, proving the price bound and the no-co-winner statement
together.  Both are immediate when $L=\varnothing$.

Assume the result for every smaller family.  Suppose that $(\{g\},y)$ and a
low report $(\{g'\},\lambda)\in L$ both win.  Put
\[
  L'=L\setminus\{(\{g'\},\lambda)\},
  \qquad
  \gamma=c_{\{H_h,(\{g\},y)\}\cup L'}(\{g'\}).
\]
Because the low report wins, $\gamma\le\lambda<y$.  Raise its bid from
$\lambda$ to $y$.
Its own critical price remains $\gamma$, so it still wins.  Swapping goods
$g$ and $g'$ fixes $H_h$ and every report in $L'$; goods equivariance
therefore gives
\[
  c_{\{H_h,(\{g'\},y)\}\cup L'}(\{g\})=\gamma.
\]
Thus, against the smaller external profile $\{H_h\}\cup L'$, the two reports
$(\{g\},y)$ and $(\{g'\},y)$ both strictly win and pay $\gamma$ each.  Treat
them as two aliases of one bidder with true target $\{g\}$.  The cover
inequality and the induction hypothesis imply
\[
  t
  \le c_{\{H_h\}\cup L'}(\{g\})
  \le 2\gamma
  \le 2\lambda
  <t,
\]
a contradiction.  Hence a high singleton has no low co-winner.

Now choose
\[
  y>\max\{\lambda,c_{\{H_h\}\cup L}(\{g\})\}.
\]
The high singleton wins, and the statement just proved makes every report in
$L$ lose.  Regard the principal and the reports in $L$ as one false-name
family against $H_h$.  Only the principal pays, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}(b) gives
\[
  t=c_{\{H_h\}}(\{g\})
  \le c_{\{H_h\}\cup L}(\{g\}).
\]
This proves the price bound and completes the induction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic upper bound without IIG]
\label{thm:no-iig-upper}
Every mechanism in the deterministic class above satisfies
\[
  \rho(\mathcal M)\le\frac{2}{m+1}.
\]
The bound already holds on single-minded star profiles.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The claim is immediate if $\rho(\mathcal M)=0$, so assume the ratio is
positive and $m\ge2$.  Fix $\eps>0$ and choose the near-flat pair $h<h'$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-iig-flat}.  Write
\[
  t=t(h),
  \qquad
  t'=t(h'),
  \qquad
  \bar t=\max\{t,t'\}.
\]
Then $\bar t\le(1+\eps)t$.  Choose $a>\bar t$ and define the clean reverse
price
\[
  \eta(a)=c_{\{(\{1\},a)\}}(M).
\]
Because $a>t'$, the singleton $(\{1\},a)$ strictly wins against $H_{h'}$.
The grand-bundle report of value $h'$ therefore loses, so
\begin{equation}
  \eta(a)\ge h'>h.
  \label{eq:reverse-slack}
\end{equation}

Singleton auxiliaries cannot lower this grand-bundle price.  Indeed, for any
family $L$ of singleton reports, choose a grand-bundle bid above the finite
threshold $c_{\{(\{1\},a)\}\cup L}(M)$.  The grand report wins and every
singleton in $L$ loses.  Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}(b) to this principal
and the losing auxiliaries, against external report $(\{1\},a)$, gives
\begin{equation}
  c_{\{(\{1\},a)\}\cup L}(M)\ge\eta(a).
  \label{eq:dirty-grand-price}
\end{equation}

Fix $0<\lambda<t/2$ and let
\[
  L=\{(\{g\},\lambda):g=2,\ldots,m\}.
\]
Consider the star profile
\begin{equation}
  \theta^\star=\{H_h,(\{1\},a)\}\cup L.
  \label{eq:no-iig-star}
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:reverse-slack} and \eqref{eq:dirty-grand-price}, the grand bidder
faces a price strictly above $h$ and loses.

We next show that the high singleton must win.  Suppose instead that it loses
and that a low singleton $(\{g\},\lambda)$ wins.  Let
$L'=L\setminus\{(\{g\},\lambda)\}$.  Half-price persistence gives
\[
  c_{\{H_h\}\cup L'}(\{g\})\ge t.
\]
Against the external profile $\{H_h\}\cup L'$, let one physical bidder submit
the two reports $(\{g\},\lambda)$ and $(\{1\},a)$.  At $\theta^\star$, the
first wins and the second loses.  The false-name family covers $\{g\}$ and
pays at most $\lambda<t$,
contradicting Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}(b).  Thus no low singleton can win when
the high singleton loses.  Since the grand bidder already loses, this would
leave zero welfare on a positive-value profile, contradicting the assumed
positive ratio.  Hence $(\{1\},a)$ wins.

Finally, Lemma~\ref{lem:no-iig-half} implies that none of the low singletons
co-wins with $(\{1\},a)$.  Therefore
\[
  \SW_{\mathcal M}(\theta^\star)=a,
  \qquad
  \OPT(\theta^\star)\ge a+(m-1)\lambda,
\]
and
\[
  \rho(\mathcal M)
  \le\frac{a}{a+(m-1)\lambda}.
\]
Let $a\downarrow\bar t$ and $\lambda\uparrow t/2$.  Since
$\bar t\le(1+\eps)t$,
\[
  \rho(\mathcal M)
  \le
  \frac{2(1+\eps)}{2(1+\eps)+m-1}.
\]
Letting $\eps\downarrow0$ gives $2/(m+1)$.  For $m=1$, the statement reduces
to the trivial bound $\rho\le1$.
\end{proof}

The bound is tight: the Adaptive Reserve Price mechanism of
\cite{iwasaki2010worst} attains $2/(m+1)$.

\section{A randomized mechanism}
\label{sec:randomized}

Randomization removes the linear barrier of
Section~\ref{sec:deterministic}. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}(a), false-name-proofness in
expectation only requires the cover inequality to hold on average.
Starting from the greedy mechanism of \citet{lehmann2002truth},
there are two distinct obstacles to false-name-proofness with
sublinear welfare loss:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Mutual shielding.} In greedy packing with critical
payments, two aliases can redundantly displace the same competitor
so that both individual critical payments drop to zero.
\item \emph{Bundle-size discounting.} Good welfare requires
demoting large targets relative to small ones, yet a purely
formulaic discount such as \(b_i/\sqrt{|S_i|}\) breaks
false-name-proofness: a bidder can isolate contested goods in a
small alias while shifting uncontested goods into a separate alias
that pays a lower rate.
\end{enumerate}

Our construction resolves these two obstacles modularly in three
pieces. First, a \emph{reserve-greedy} base mechanism
\(\mathcal A^{\mu}\) (Section~\ref{sec:rand-reserve}) runs
unweighted greedy and taxes winners with a random reserve drawn as
a fraction of the largest rejected bid; we characterize the
admissible reserve laws \(\mu\) via a single truncated-expectation
condition~\eqref{eq:R} and link it to \citet{myerson1981optimal}.
Second, to implement bundle-size discounting without breaking
false-name-proofness, \emph{physical thinning}
(Section~\ref{sec:rand-thinning}) provides a black-box reduction:
deleting goods according to any independent random subset
\(\widetilde M\subseteq M\) and scoring bids by their survival
probabilities \(q(S)=\Pr[S\subseteq\widetilde M]\) preserves
universal truthfulness, ex-post IR, NPT, and false-name-proofness
in expectation, while translating expected welfare directly to the
scored profile. Third, mixing thinned
reserve-greedy mechanisms across logarithmically many geometric
retention scales (Section~\ref{sec:rand-welfare}) turns each scale
into a size filter, allowing the classical charging argument of
\citet[Theorem~3]{lehmann2002truth} to yield an
\(O(\sqrt m\log m)\) approximation.

We present the concrete mechanism and main theorem up front
(Section~\ref{sec:rand-mechanism}), prove the two general
incentive modules (Sections~\ref{sec:rand-reserve}
and~\ref{sec:rand-thinning}), establish the welfare bound
(Section~\ref{sec:rand-welfare}), and conclude with symmetry,
runtime, and an exact finite-precision implementation that
preserves universal truthfulness
(Section~\ref{sec:rand-implementation}).

\subsection{The mechanism and main result}
\label{sec:rand-mechanism}

\paragraph{Greedy notation.}
A \emph{weighted report} \((S_i,w_i)\) consists of a nonempty
target \(S_i\subseteq M\) and a weight \(w_i\ge0\). For a finite
indexed profile \(T\) of weighted reports, let \(G(T)\) be the
greedy packing obtained by scanning reports in nonincreasing order
of weight and accepting a report whenever its target is disjoint
from the previously accepted targets. Ties are broken by a
priority order \(\omega\) on identities, independent of values and
targets, with every subprofile inheriting the induced order.
Define the \emph{greedy normalizer} (the largest rejected weight)
\[
C(T)=\max_{i\in T\setminus G(T)}w_i,
\]
with \(\max\varnothing=0\). For a weighted profile \(E\) and a
nonempty bundle \(R\subseteq M\), define
\[
\kappa(R;E)=
\max\bigl\{w_j:j\in G(E),\ S_j\cap R\ne\varnothing\bigr\},
\qquad
B(R;E)=\max\{C(E),\,\kappa(R;E)\},
\]
again with \(\max\varnothing=0\). The \emph{greedy critical
weight} of \(i\in G(T)\) is
\(\kappa_i(T)=\kappa(S_i;T\setminus\{i\})\).

\paragraph{Dyadic scales.}
Let
\[
\mathcal K=\bigl\{1,2,4,\ldots,2^{\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor}\bigr\},
\qquad
|\mathcal K|=1+\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor.
\]
A \emph{scale} is an element \(k\in\mathcal K\cup\{\infty\}\). At
scale \(k\), each good is retained independently with probability
\[
p_k=1-\frac{1}{2k},
\qquad
p_\infty=1,
\]
so a bundle \(R\subseteq M\) survives with probability
\(q_k(R)=p_k^{|R|}\).

\begin{definition}[Fixed-scale mechanism \(\mathcal A_k\) and mixture \(\mathcal M\)]
\label{def:M}
Fix a scale \(k\in\mathcal K\cup\{\infty\}\). Given a report
profile \((S_i,b_i)\), the mechanism \(\mathcal A_k\) runs four
steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Survival scoring.} Assign report \(i\) the weight
\(w_i=q_k(S_i)b_i\), forming the weighted profile \(T\).
\item \emph{Greedy packing.} Draw a uniformly random priority
order \(\omega\) on the participating identities, independently of
the reports, and compute
\[
G=G(T),\qquad
C=C(T),\qquad
\kappa_i=\kappa_i(T)\quad(i\in G).
\]
\item \emph{Random reserve and deletions.} Draw
\(\xi\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\). Independently, form the set
of surviving goods \(\widetilde M\subseteq M\) by retaining each
good independently with probability \(p_k\).
\item \emph{Allocation and payments.} Serve bidder \(i\) with its
target \(S_i\) precisely when
\[
i\in G,\qquad w_i\ge\xi C,\qquad S_i\subseteq\widetilde M,
\]
and then charge
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual-payment}
P_i=\frac{\max\{\kappa_i,\,\xi C\}}{q_k(S_i)}.
\end{equation}
Every other bidder receives nothing and pays zero.
\end{enumerate}
The mechanism \(\mathcal M\) runs \(\mathcal A_\infty\) with
probability \(1/2\); with probability \(1/2\), it draws a scale
\(k\) uniformly from \(\mathcal K\) and runs \(\mathcal A_k\).
\end{definition}

\begin{observation}\label{obs:thinning-view}
At scale \(\infty\), no good is deleted (\(p_\infty=1\)) and
weights equal raw bids. For each finite \(k\in\mathcal K\),
\(\mathcal A_k\) is the thinning (Definition~\ref{def:thinning})
of the unweighted reserve-greedy mechanism \(\mathcal A_\infty\)
by \(\widetilde M\): it scores each report by its expected
surviving value, runs \(\mathcal A_\infty\), withholds any bundle
that loses a deleted good, and divides winning payments by
\(q_k(S_i)\) so expected payments match \(\mathcal A_\infty\).
\end{observation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For every \(m\ge1\), the mechanism \(\mathcal M\) of
Definition~\ref{def:M} for single-minded bidders is ex-post
feasible, universally truthful, false-name-proof in expectation,
ex-post individually rational, and makes no positive transfers.
For every type profile \(\theta\),
\[
\E[\SW_{\mathcal M}(\theta)]
\ge
\frac{\OPT(\theta)}{
4\sqrt{2m\ln(em)\bigl(1+\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor\bigr)}+8/e}.
\]
In particular, \(1/\rho(\mathcal M)=O(\sqrt m\log(m+1))\).
The mechanism is anonymous in utilities and symmetric across
goods, and runs in polynomial time (using \(O(n^2\log n+n^2m)\)
arithmetic operations, and polynomial bit complexity under the
discrete reserve of Section~\ref{sec:rand-implementation}). No
independence-of-irrelevant-goods condition is imposed.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} follows immediately by combining the
incentive guarantees of Corollary~\ref{cor:mixture}, the welfare
bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:welfare}, and the symmetry and
complexity bounds of Proposition~\ref{prop:implementation}.
Moreover, Section~\ref{sec:rand-implementation} shows that
replacing \(\xi\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\) by a discrete
uniform draw on \(\{1/m^2,2/m^2,\ldots,1\}\) yields exact
polynomial-bit rational payments while preserving universal
truthfulness and the \(O(\sqrt m\log(m+1))\) welfare bound.

\subsection{Reserve-greedy mechanisms}
\label{sec:rand-reserve}

We first analyze the unweighted base mechanism, without deletions,
for an arbitrary reserve distribution.

\begin{definition}[Reserve-greedy mechanism \(\mathcal A^{\mu}\)]
\label{def:reserve-greedy}
Let \(\mu\) be a probability law on \([0,1]\). Given reports
\((S_i,b_i)\), set weights \(w_i=b_i\) to form the weighted
profile \(T\), draw a uniformly random priority order \(\omega\)
and an independent reserve fraction \(\xi\sim\mu\), and compute
\(G=G(T)\), \(C=C(T)\), and \(\kappa_i=\kappa_i(T)\). Serve
bidder \(i\) with \(S_i\) precisely when \(i\in G\) and
\(w_i\ge\xi C\), charging \(P_i=\max\{\kappa_i,\xi C\}\); every
other bidder receives nothing and pays zero.
\end{definition}

In particular, \(\mathcal A_\infty=\mathcal A^{\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]}\).
The truthfulness and false-name analysis of \(\mathcal A^{\mu}\)
rests on two pure properties of greedy independent sets in the
conflict graph of \(T\) (where two reports share an edge whenever
their targets intersect).

\begin{lemma}[Conflict-graph normalizer]
\label{lem:greedy}
Let \(T\) and \(E\) be weighted profiles.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] The normalizer equals the largest minimum weight of a
conflicting pair:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair}
C(T)=
\max_{\substack{i,j\in T,\ i\ne j\\
                 S_i\cap S_j\ne\varnothing}}
\min\{w_i,w_j\}.
\end{equation}
In particular, \(C(T)\) does not depend on the priority order and
cannot decrease when reports are added.
\item[(b)] Add a report \((R,w)\) to \(E\) under a new identity.
It is accepted by greedy if \(w>\kappa(R;E)\) and rejected if
\(w<\kappa(R;E)\). Whenever it is accepted,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalizer}
C\bigl(E\cup\{(R,w)\}\bigr)=B(R;E).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Because \(G(T)\) is an independent set in the conflict graph,
every conflicting pair has at least one rejected member, whose
weight is at most \(C(T)\); hence the right-hand side
of~\eqref{eq:pair} is at most \(C(T)\). Conversely, every rejected
report is blocked by an earlier accepted neighbor of at least its
weight, so the minimum weight of that conflicting pair equals the
rejected weight. This gives the reverse inequality.

(b) Reports processed before \((R,w)\) face the same decisions as
in \(G(E)\), so \((R,w)\) is accepted if and only if no report of
\(G(E)\) processed before it intersects \(R\). This holds when
\(w>\kappa(R;E)\) and fails when \(w<\kappa(R;E)\).

Suppose \((R,w)\) is accepted, so \(w\ge\kappa(R;E)\). Every
conflicting pair inside \(E\) has minimum weight at most \(C(E)\).
For any \(j\in E\) conflicting with \(R\), either \(j\in G(E)\),
so \(\min\{w,w_j\}=w_j\le\kappa(R;E)\), or \(j\notin G(E)\), so
\(\min\{w,w_j\}\le w_j\le C(E)\). Equation~\eqref{eq:pair}
therefore gives \(C(E\cup\{(R,w)\})\le B(R;E)\). The reverse
inequality holds by monotonicity of \(C\) and, when
\(\kappa(R;E)>0\), by the conflicting pair formed by \((R,w)\) and
a report of \(G(E)\) of weight \(\kappa(R;E)\).
\end{proof}

On a single good, the conflict graph is complete and
\(C(T)\) is simply the second-highest bid;
Lemma~\ref{lem:greedy}(b) shows that \(C(T)\) retains the key
property of the second-highest bid on arbitrary conflict graphs:
for every greedy winner, it is independent of that winner's own
bid and nondecreasing in its target.

\begin{lemma}[Ex-post properties of \(\mathcal A^{\mu}\)]
\label{lem:threshold}
For every reserve law \(\mu\) on \([0,1]\), the mechanism
\(\mathcal A^{\mu}\) is ex-post feasible, universally truthful,
ex-post individually rational, and makes no positive transfers.
For every realization of \((\omega,\xi)\), external profile \(E\),
and nonempty bundle \(R\subseteq M\), the realized critical price
is
\[
c_E(R)=\max\{\kappa(R;E),\,\xi B(R;E)\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Served bidders form a subset of \(G(T)\), and payments are
nonnegative. Fix \((\omega,\xi)\) and \(E\). By
Lemma~\ref{lem:greedy}(b), a report \((R,b)\) against \(E\) is
accepted by greedy if \(b>\kappa(R;E)\) and rejected if
\(b<\kappa(R;E)\); whenever accepted, its greedy critical weight
is \(\kappa(R;E)\) and the normalizer of \(E\cup\{(R,b)\}\) is
\(B(R;E)\). An accepted report therefore clears the reserve
precisely when \(b\ge\xi B(R;E)\), and then pays
\(\max\{\kappa(R;E),\xi B(R;E)\}=c_E(R)\). Thus \((R,b)\) wins and
pays \(c_E(R)\) when \(b>c_E(R)\), loses when \(b<c_E(R)\), and at
equality either loses or wins at payment \(b\). Moreover,
enlarging \(R\) cannot decrease \(\kappa(R;E)\) or \(B(R;E)\), so
\(c_E(R)\) is nondecreasing in \(R\) and independent of \(b\).

Each realization is therefore an exact, bid- and
bundle-monotone critical-price mechanism---a price-oriented,
rationing-free protocol \citep{yokoo2003characterization}---and is
consequently DSIC and ex-post individually rational
\citep[Theorem~4]{lehmann2002truth}; see also
\citet{mualem2008truthful}. Averaging over \((\omega,\xi)\) yields
universal truthfulness \citep{dobzinski2006truthful}.
\end{proof}

For false names, the only combinatorial input is the following
dichotomy: greedy packing reduces any multi-alias displacement of
an external blocker---including indirect cascades of arbitrary
length---to either one unshielded alias or at least two heavy
aliases.

\begin{lemma}[Displacement dichotomy]
\label{lem:displacement}
Let \(E\) be a weighted profile, let \(S\subseteq M\) be a
nonempty bundle, and put \(\kappa=\kappa(S;E)\) and
\(B=B(S;E)\). Let \(T'\) be obtained from \(E\) by adding
finitely many weighted reports under new identities \(A\), with a
priority order extending that of \(E\). Let \(F\subseteq A\) be
the set of new reports accepted in \(G(T')\), and suppose that the
targets of \(F\) cover \(S\). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-comparison}
C(T')\ge B.
\end{equation}
Moreover, if \(\kappa>0\), then one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] at least two reports of \(F\) have weight at least
\(\kappa\);
\item[(b)] exactly one report \(j\in F\) has weight at least
\(\kappa\), and \(\kappa_j(T')\ge\kappa\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:greedy}(a), \(C(T')\ge C(E)\). If \(\kappa>0\),
fix a report of \(G(E)\) of weight \(\kappa\) whose target
intersects \(S\) (the \emph{blocker}). Because the targets of
\(F\subseteq G(T')\) cover \(S\) and \(G(T')\) is disjoint, the
blocker is rejected in \(T'\), giving \(C(T')\ge\kappa\) and
proving~\eqref{eq:C-comparison}.

Suppose now that \(\kappa>0\) and~(a) fails. Since \(F\ne\varnothing\),
pick \(j\in F\) such that every report in \(F\setminus\{j\}\) has
weight strictly below \(\kappa\). We show that
\(\kappa_j(T')\ge\kappa\); since \(j\in G(T')\),
Lemma~\ref{lem:greedy}(b) then forces \(w_j\ge\kappa_j(T')\ge\kappa\),
yielding~(b).

Assume for contradiction that \(\kappa_j(T')<\kappa\): in
\(T'\setminus\{j\}\), no accepted report of weight at least
\(\kappa\) intersects \(S_j\). Call all reports of weight at least
\(\kappa\) the \emph{prefix}; greedy processes the prefix before
any lighter report. We claim that \(G(T')\) and
\(G(T'\setminus\{j\})\) accept the same prefix reports other than
\(j\). This is immediate if \(w_j<\kappa\). Otherwise, scanning
the prefix in greedy order, the two runs agree before \(j\),
\(j\) is accepted in \(G(T')\), and every later prefix report
accepted in \(G(T'\setminus\{j\})\) is disjoint from \(S_j\) by
assumption, so \(j\) alters no subsequent prefix decision.

By the claim, any alias in \(A\setminus\{j\}\) accepted in the
prefix of \(G(T'\setminus\{j\})\) would belong to
\(F\setminus\{j\}\) and have weight at least \(\kappa\), which is
impossible. Thus all aliases in the prefix of
\(T'\setminus\{j\}\) are rejected. Deleting rejected reports never
changes greedy, so the prefix of \(G(T'\setminus\{j\})\) coincides
with the prefix of \(G(E)\). In particular, the blocker is
accepted in \(G(T'\setminus\{j\})\) and, by the claim, in
\(G(T')\), contradicting the first paragraph.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Admissible reserve laws]
\label{prop:reserve}
Let \(\mu\) be a probability law on \([0,1]\), let \(\xi\sim\mu\),
and consider the condition
\begin{equation*}\label{eq:R}
\tag{R}
\E\bigl[\xi\,\mathbf{1}\{\xi\le\beta\}\bigr]
\ge\frac{\beta}{2}\Pr[\xi\le\beta]
\qquad\text{for every }\beta\in[0,1].
\end{equation*}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If \(\mu\) satisfies~\eqref{eq:R}, then
\(\mathcal A^{\mu}\) satisfies the cover
inequality~\eqref{eq:cover-general} and is therefore
false-name-proof in expectation.
\item[(b)] Conversely, if \(m\ge3\) and \(\mathcal A^{\mu}\) is
false-name-proof in expectation, then \(\mu\)
satisfies~\eqref{eq:R}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) By Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:cover}(a), it suffices to verify the cover
inequality~\eqref{eq:cover-general} conditional on any priority
order of \(E\) and arbitrary priorities of the aliases \(A\). Fix
a target \(S\), external profile \(E\), and alias family
\(\hat\theta_A\) with weights \(w_j\), forming \(T'\). Put
\(\kappa=\kappa(S;E)\), \(B=B(S;E)\), \(C'=C(T')\), and let
\(F=A\cap G(T')\). If the targets of \(F\) do not cover \(S\), the
right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:cover-general} is zero and there is
nothing to prove. Assume henceforth that they cover \(S\), and set
\[
F_S=\{j\in F:S_j\cap S\ne\varnothing\},
\qquad
\alpha=\min_{j\in F_S}\min\{w_j,C'\},
\]
with the minimum attained by \(j_0\in F_S\).

For any realization of \(\xi\in[0,1]\), an alias \(j\in F\) is
served precisely when \(\xi C'\le\min\{w_j,C'\}\), paying
\(\max\{\kappa_j(T'),\xi C'\}\). Because the targets of \(F\) are
disjoint, every alias in \(F_S\) is required to cover \(S\), so
the served aliases cover \(S\) only when \(\xi C'\le\alpha\).
Since \(C'\ge B\) by~\eqref{eq:C-comparison} and
\(c_E(S)=\max\{\kappa,\xi B\}\) by Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold}, it
suffices to prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cover}
\E_\xi\Bigl[\,
\sum_{j\in F}
\mathbf{1}\{\xi C'\le\min\{w_j,C'\}\}
\max\{\kappa_j(T'),\xi C'\}
\Bigr]
\ge
\E_\xi\bigl[
\mathbf{1}\{\xi C'\le\alpha\}\max\{\kappa,\xi C'\}
\bigr].
\end{equation}
We verify~\eqref{eq:cover} in three cases.

\emph{Case 1: \(\kappa=0\).} Pointwise in \(\xi\), alias \(j_0\)
pays at least \(\mathbf{1}\{\xi C'\le\alpha\}\,\xi C'\), matching
the right-hand integrand.

\emph{Case 2: \(\kappa>0\) and Lemma~\ref{lem:displacement}(b)
holds.} Let \(j\in F\) be the unique alias of weight at least
\(\kappa\), so \(\kappa_j(T')\ge\kappa\). Then
\(\min\{w_j,C'\}\ge\alpha\) (with equality if \(j=j_0\), and
otherwise \(\alpha\le w_{j_0}<\kappa\le\min\{w_j,C'\}\)).
Pointwise in \(\xi\), alias \(j\) alone pays at least
\(\mathbf{1}\{\xi C'\le\alpha\}\max\{\kappa,\xi C'\}\).

\emph{Case 3: \(\kappa>0\) and Lemma~\ref{lem:displacement}(a)
holds.} Call aliases in \(F\) of weight at least \(\kappa\)
\emph{heavy}; there are at least two, and
\(\beta:=\kappa/C'\in(0,1]\). Every heavy alias clears \(\xi C'\)
whenever \(\xi\le\beta\), paying at least \(\xi C'\). If
\(\alpha\ge\kappa\), pick a heavy alias \(j\ne j_0\); the payments
of \(j_0\) and \(j\) total at least
\(\mathbf{1}\{\xi C'\le\alpha\}\,\xi C'
+\mathbf{1}\{\xi\le\beta\}\,\xi C'\), while
\(\mathbf{1}\{\xi C'\le\alpha\}\max\{\kappa,\xi C'\}
=\mathbf{1}\{\xi\le\beta\}\,\kappa
+\mathbf{1}\{\kappa<\xi C'\le\alpha\}\,\xi C'\).
If \(\alpha<\kappa\), two heavy aliases pay at least
\(2\,\mathbf{1}\{\xi\le\beta\}\,\xi C'\), while the right-hand
integrand is at most \(\mathbf{1}\{\xi\le\beta\}\,\kappa\). In
both subcases, the left-hand integrand exceeds the right-hand
integrand by at least \(\mathbf{1}\{\xi\le\beta\}(2\xi-\beta)C'\),
whose expectation is nonnegative by~\eqref{eq:R}.

(b) Suppose~\eqref{eq:R} fails at some \(\beta\in(0,1]\). On goods
\(1,2,3\), let \(E\) consist of two reports \((\{3\},1)\) and one
report \((\{1,2\},\beta)\), so \(C(E)=1\),
\(\kappa(\{1,2\};E)=\beta\), and \(B(\{1,2\};E)=1\). A truthful
bidder of type \((\{1,2\},\beta)\) has utility
\(\max\{0,\beta-\max\{\beta,\xi\}\}=0\) identically. Submitting
two aliases \((\{1\},\beta')\) and \((\{2\},\beta')\) with
\(\beta'>\beta\) displaces \((\{1,2\},\beta)\), leaving \(C'=1\)
and \(\kappa_1=\kappa_2=0\). Both aliases are served whenever
\(\xi\le\beta'\) and each pays \(\xi\), yielding expected utility
\(\beta\Pr[\xi\le\beta']-2\,\E[\xi\,\mathbf{1}\{\xi\le\beta'\}]\).
As \(\beta'\downarrow\beta\), this converges to
\(\beta\Pr[\xi\le\beta]-2\,\E[\xi\,\mathbf{1}\{\xi\le\beta\}]>0\),
violating false-name-proofness in expectation.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Myerson interpretation and canonical reserve laws.}
Let \(F(\beta)=\Pr[\xi\le\beta]\) be the distribution function of
\(\mu\). By Fubini's theorem,
\[
\E\bigl[\xi\,\mathbf{1}\{\xi\le\beta\}\bigr]
=\beta F(\beta)-\int_0^\beta F(\zeta)\,d\zeta,
\]
which is the expected payment associated with the monotone
allocation probability \(F\) by \citet{myerson1981optimal}.
Condition~\eqref{eq:R} is therefore equivalent to
\(\int_0^\beta F(\zeta)\,d\zeta\le\frac{\beta}{2}F(\beta)\): on
every level \([0,\beta]\), Myerson surplus must not exceed Myerson
payment, so two shielding aliases together pay at least the full
bid times service probability. Equivalently,
\(\beta^{-2}\int_0^\beta F(\zeta)\,d\zeta\) is nondecreasing on
\((0,1]\), so every admissible law satisfies
\(\int_0^\beta F(\zeta)\,d\zeta\le\beta^2/2\). Three examples
illuminate the boundary:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Continuous uniform law \(\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\).}
It is the unique law attaining equality in~\eqref{eq:R} at every
\(\beta\in[0,1]\), serving low bids as frequently as~\eqref{eq:R}
permits.
\item \emph{Deterministic reserve \(\xi\equiv\beta_0\).}
It satisfies~\eqref{eq:R} if and only if \(\beta_0\ge1/2\). Every
greedy winner below \(C/2\) is then excluded, explaining why
deterministic reserves encounter the \((m+1)/2\) welfare barrier
of Section~\ref{sec:deterministic} and \citet{iwasaki2010worst}.
\item \emph{Discrete uniform law \(\operatorname{Unif}\{1/M,2/M,\ldots,1\}\).}
For every integer \(M\ge1\) and every \(\beta\in[0,1]\), putting
\(k=\lfloor\beta M\rfloor\) gives \(\beta<(k+1)/M\) and hence
\[
\E\bigl[\xi\,\mathbf{1}\{\xi\le\beta\}\bigr]
=\frac{k(k+1)}{2M^2}
\ge\frac{\beta k}{2M}
=\frac{\beta}{2}\Pr[\xi\le\beta].
\]
Every finite uniform grid therefore satisfies~\eqref{eq:R}; we use
this in Section~\ref{sec:rand-implementation} for exact rational
arithmetic without giving up universal truthfulness.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Physical thinning and scale mixtures}
\label{sec:rand-thinning}

Unweighted reserve-greedy \(\mathcal A_\infty\) is
false-name-proof, but a single grand-bundle bid of value \(1\)
still blocks \(m\) disjoint singletons of value \(1-\eps\),
leaving linear welfare loss. Simply dividing bids by
\(\sqrt{|S_i|}\) (as in \citet{lehmann2002truth}) repairs welfare
but destroys false-name-proofness: against a single rival
\((\{1\},1)\), a grand bidder splits into \((\{1\},1+\eps)\) and
\((\{2,\ldots,m\},\sqrt{m-1}(1+\eps))\), paying \(1\) on \(\{1\}\)
and only \(\frac12\sqrt{m-1}\) on average for the uncontested
goods, strictly below the truthful price \(\sqrt m\). Physical
thinning resolves this by paying the size discount in real
survival probability.

Following \citet{lehmann2002truth}, call a mechanism \emph{exact}
if every bidder receives either its reported target or nothing,
with unserved bidders paying zero. We formulate thinning for an
arbitrary exact base mechanism \(\mathcal M_0\); in our
construction we apply it to the false-name-proof reserve-greedy
mechanism \(\mathcal M_0=\mathcal A^{\mu}\) of
Section~\ref{sec:rand-reserve}.

\begin{definition}[Physical thinning]
\label{def:thinning}
Let \(\mathcal M_0\) be an exact mechanism, and let
\(\widetilde M\subseteq M\) be a random set of \emph{surviving}
goods, drawn independently of the reports and of the randomness of
\(\mathcal M_0\), with survival probabilities
\(q(R)=\Pr[R\subseteq\widetilde M]>0\) for all \(R\subseteq M\).
For any profile \(\hat\theta\) with reports \((S_i,b_i)\), write
\(q\hat\theta\) for the \emph{scored profile} with reports
\((S_i,q(S_i)b_i)\). The \emph{thinning} of \(\mathcal M_0\) by
\(\widetilde M\) runs \(\mathcal M_0\) on \(q\hat\theta\), serves
bidder \(i\) with \(S_i\) precisely when \(\mathcal M_0\) serves
\(i\) and \(S_i\subseteq\widetilde M\), and then charges
\(P_i/q(S_i)\), where \(P_i\) is \(i\)'s payment in
\(\mathcal M_0\). Unserved bidders pay zero.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Thinning preserves incentives]
\label{prop:thinning}
Let \(\mathcal M_0\) be an exact mechanism that makes no positive
transfers. Its thinning by \(\widetilde M\) is exact and makes no
positive transfers. Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] if \(\mathcal M_0\) is ex-post feasible (or ex-post
individually rational), then so is the thinning;
\item[(b)] if \(\mathcal M_0\) is truthful in expectation (or
false-name-proof in expectation), then so is the thinning;
\item[(c)] if \(\mathcal M_0\) is universally truthful and ex-post
individually rational, then the thinning is universally truthful;
\item[(d)] for every type profile \(\theta\), the expected welfare
of the thinning at \(\theta\) equals
\(\E[\SW_{\mathcal M_0}(q\theta)]\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The thinning allocates a subset of the bundles allocated by
\(\mathcal M_0\) and multiplies positive payments by positive
constants, preserving exactness, NPT, and ex-post feasibility.

Fix a bidder of type \((S,v)\) against an external profile \(E\),
and put \(z=q(S)v\). Compare this bidder in the thinning against
\(E\) with a \emph{fictitious} bidder of type \((S,z)\) in
\(\mathcal M_0\) against \(qE\).

\emph{Truthful reports and~(a), (d).} Under truthful reporting,
\(\mathcal M_0\) runs on \((S,z)\) against \(qE\), generating
tentative payment \(P\). The bidder's realized utility in the
thinning is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thin-truthful}
\mathbf{1}\{S\subseteq\widetilde M\}
\left(v\,\mathbf{1}\{\text{served in }\mathcal M_0\}-\frac{P}{q(S)}\right)
=\frac{\mathbf{1}\{S\subseteq\widetilde M\}}{q(S)}
\bigl(z\,\mathbf{1}\{\text{served in }\mathcal M_0\}-P\bigr),
\end{equation}
a nonnegative multiple of the fictitious bidder's realized utility
in \(\mathcal M_0\), proving ex-post IR in~(a). Since
\(\widetilde M\) is independent of \(\mathcal M_0\), taking
expectations shows that both bidders have the same truthful
expected utility and that the bidder is served in the thinning
with probability \(q(S)\) times its service probability in
\(\mathcal M_0\). Multiplying by \(v_i\) and summing over bidders
gives~(d).

\emph{False names and~(b).} Replace the truthful report by aliases
\((S_j,b_j)\), \(j\in A\), so \(\mathcal M_0\) runs on
\((S_j,q(S_j)b_j)\) against \(qE\) and serves a random subset
\(D\subseteq A\) with payments \(P_j\). Because deleted goods are
never allocated, the aliases cover \(S\) in the thinning only if
both \(S\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in D}S_j\) and
\(S\subseteq\widetilde M\). By independence, the bidder's expected
value is at most
\[
v\,q(S)\Pr\Bigl[S\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in D}S_j\Bigr]
=z\Pr\Bigl[S\subseteq\bigcup_{j\in D}S_j\Bigr].
\]
Each alias \(j\in D\) pays \(P_j/q(S_j)\) with conditional
probability \(\Pr[S_j\subseteq\widetilde M]=q(S_j)\), so its
expected payment equals \(\E[P_j\mathbf{1}\{j\in D\}]\), its
expected payment in \(\mathcal M_0\). Hence the deviation's
expected utility in the thinning is at most the fictitious
bidder's expected utility from the scored aliases in
\(\mathcal M_0\), which by FNP of \(\mathcal M_0\) is at most the
fictitious truthful utility---equal to the thinning's truthful
utility. Taking \(|A|=1\) gives truthfulness, proving~(b).

\emph{Universal truthfulness and~(c).} Fix a realization of
\(\mathcal M_0\) and \(\widetilde M\), and consider any single
report \((R,b)\). If it is not served in the thinning or
\(S\not\subseteq R\), its realized utility is at most zero, hence
at most the truthful realized utility by~(a). Otherwise
\(S\subseteq R\subseteq\widetilde M\), so \(q(R)\le q(S)\), and
\((R,q(R)b)\) is served in \(\mathcal M_0\) at some payment
\(P\ge0\). The realized utility is
\[
v-\frac{P}{q(R)}\le v-\frac{P}{q(S)}=\frac{z-P}{q(S)},
\]
which by universal truthfulness of \(\mathcal M_0\) is at most
\(q(S)^{-1}\) times the fictitious truthful realized utility---the
right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:thin-truthful} when
\(S\subseteq\widetilde M\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:mixture}
Let \(\mu\) be any reserve law on \([0,1]\) satisfying~\eqref{eq:R}.
Every mechanism that draws a scale
\(k\in\mathcal K\cup\{\infty\}\) from a fixed distribution,
independently of the reports, and runs the thinning of
\(\mathcal A^{\mu}\) by the scale-\(k\) survival set
\(\widetilde M\) is ex-post feasible, universally truthful,
ex-post individually rational, false-name-proof in expectation,
and makes no positive transfers. In particular, this holds for
\(\mathcal M\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:threshold} and Proposition~\ref{prop:reserve}(a),
\(\mathcal A^{\mu}\) has all five properties (and is exact). By
Proposition~\ref{prop:thinning}, each thinned scale mechanism
inherits them. Ex-post properties hold for every drawn scale, and
averaging expected utilities over an independent scale draw
preserves false-name-proofness in expectation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Welfare of the dyadic mixture}
\label{sec:rand-welfare}

The welfare proof adapts the charging and Cauchy--Schwarz argument
of \citet[Theorem~3]{lehmann2002truth}. In deterministic greedy,
winners satisfy \(\sum_{j\in G}w_j^2\le\SW^2\). In
\(\mathcal A_\infty\), the uniform reserve replaces \(\SW^2\) by
the product \(V\varphi\) of the maximum bid and expected welfare;
mixing \(\mathcal A_\infty\) with thinned scales closes the loop
via AM--GM.

\begin{lemma}[Second-moment bound and charging for \(\mathcal A_\infty\)]
\label{lem:welfare-base}
Let \(\theta\) be a type profile with \(\max_i v_i\le V\). Fix a
priority order \(\omega\) in \(\mathcal A_\infty\), let \(G\) and
\(C\) be the resulting greedy packing and normalizer, and let
\(\varphi\) be the expected welfare over \(\xi\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] We have
\(\varphi=\sum_{j\in G}v_j\min\{1,v_j/C\}\) (with the minimum
replaced by \(1\) if \(C=0\)), \(\varphi\ge\max_i v_i\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:squares}
\sum_{j\in G}v_j^2\le V\varphi.
\end{equation}
\item[(b)] For every set \(I\subseteq N\) of bidders with pairwise
disjoint targets and every size cutoff \(\chi\ge1\),
\[
\sum_{i\in I}v_i
\le
\sqrt{\chi\,|I|\,V\varphi}
+|I|\max\{v_j:j\in G,\ |S_j|>\chi\},
\]
with \(\max\varnothing=0\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) Each \(j\in G\) clears \(\xi C\) with probability
\(\min\{1,v_j/C\}\). The first report processed by greedy has
value \(\max_i v_i\ge C\), is accepted, and clears \(\xi C\) for
all \(\xi\), giving \(\varphi\ge\max_i v_i\). Since \(v_j\le V\)
and \(C\le V\), we have \(v_j^2\le Vv_j\min\{1,v_j/C\}\);
summing over \(j\in G\) yields~\eqref{eq:squares}.

(b) Following \citet[Theorem~3]{lehmann2002truth}, assign each
\(i\in I\) to the first winner \(j\in G\) whose target intersects
\(S_i\) (or to \(i\) itself if \(i\in G\)), and let \(\nu_j\) be
the number assigned to \(j\in G\). Greedy ordering gives
\(v_i\le v_j\), and disjointness of \(I\) gives \(\nu_j\le|S_j|\)
and \(\sum_{j\in G}\nu_j=|I|\), so
\(\sum_{i\in I}v_i\le\sum_{j\in G}\nu_j v_j\). For winners with
\(|S_j|\le\chi\), we have \(\sum\nu_j^2\le\chi\,|I|\), and
Cauchy--Schwarz with~\eqref{eq:squares} gives
\[
\sum_{\substack{j\in G\\|S_j|\le\chi}}\nu_j v_j
\le\sqrt{\chi\,|I|\,V\varphi}.
\]
Winners with \(|S_j|>\chi\) contribute at most \(|I|\) times their
largest value.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Welfare guarantee]
\label{prop:welfare}
For every type profile \(\theta\),
\[
\E[\SW_{\mathcal M}(\theta)]
\ge
\frac{\OPT(\theta)}{
4\sqrt{2m\ln(em)\,|\mathcal K|}+8/e}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Put \(V=\max_i v_i\); the claim is trivial if \(V=0\). For each
scale \(k\in\mathcal K\cup\{\infty\}\), let \(\Phi_k\) be the
expected welfare of \(\mathcal A_k\) at \(\theta\). By
Proposition~\ref{prop:thinning}(d), \(\Phi_k\) equals the expected
welfare of \(\mathcal A_\infty\) on the scored profile
\(q_k\theta\), whose weights \(w_i=q_k(S_i)v_i\) are at most
\(V\). Writing \(\varphi_{k,\omega}\) for the expected welfare of
\(\mathcal A_\infty\) on \(q_k\theta\) over \(\xi\) at fixed
\(\omega\), we have \(\Phi_k=\E_\omega[\varphi_{k,\omega}]\).

At scale \(\infty\), Lemma~\ref{lem:welfare-base}(a) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:top}
\Phi_\infty\ge V.
\end{equation}

For the finite scales, fix an optimal disjoint packing
\(N^\star\subseteq N\) of positive-value bidders with total value
\(\OPT(\theta)\), and
partition \(N^\star\) into the dyadic size classes
\(N_k=\{i\in N^\star:k\le|S_i|<2k\}\) for \(k\in\mathcal K\). Put
\(W_k=\sum_{i\in N_k}v_i\), \(n_k=|N_k|\), and
\(m_k=\sum_{i\in N_k}|S_i|\). Disjointness gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass}
k\,n_k\le m_k,\qquad
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}m_k\le m,\qquad
\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}n_k\le m.
\end{equation}
For \(i\in N_k\), since \(|S_i|<2k\) and \((1-1/(2k))^{2k}\) is
nondecreasing in \(k\ge1\),
\[
q_k(S_i)\ge\left(1-\frac{1}{2k}\right)^{2k}
\ge\left(1-\frac12\right)^2=\frac14,
\]
so \(v_i\le4w_i\). Set \(d=\ln(em)\) and apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:welfare-base}(b) to \(q_k\theta\) with \(I=N_k\)
and \(\chi=2kd\). Every winner \(j\) with \(|S_j|>2kd\) satisfies
\[
w_j\le q_k(S_j)V\le e^{-|S_j|/(2k)}V<\frac{V}{em}.
\]
Using~\eqref{eq:mass},
\[
\frac{W_k}{4}
\le\sum_{i\in N_k}w_i
\le\sqrt{2kd\,n_k V\varphi_{k,\omega}}+\frac{Vn_k}{em}
\le\sqrt{2d\,m_k V\varphi_{k,\omega}}+\frac{Vn_k}{em}.
\]
Averaging over \(\omega\) and using concavity of the square root
yields
\[
W_k\le4\sqrt{2d\,m_k V\Phi_k}+\frac{4Vn_k}{em}.
\]
Summing over \(k\in\mathcal K\) and applying Cauchy--Schwarz
with~\eqref{eq:mass},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:opt}
\OPT(\theta)
\le 4\sqrt{2dV}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\sqrt{m_k\Phi_k}+\frac{4V}{e}
\le 4\sqrt{2mdV\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\Phi_k}+\frac{4V}{e}.
\end{equation}

Finally, by Definition~\ref{def:M} and~\eqref{eq:top}, the
expected welfare \(\Phi=\E[\SW_{\mathcal M}(\theta)]\) satisfies
\[
\Phi
=\frac12\Phi_\infty+\frac{1}{2|\mathcal K|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\Phi_k
\ge\frac12 V+\frac{1}{2|\mathcal K|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\Phi_k.
\]
By the arithmetic--geometric mean inequality,
\(V\sum_{k\in\mathcal K}\Phi_k\le|\mathcal K|\,\Phi^2\) and
\(V\le2\Phi\). Substituting into~\eqref{eq:opt} gives
\[
\OPT(\theta)
\le\left(4\sqrt{2m\ln(em)\,|\mathcal K|}+\frac8e\right)\Phi.
\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{Symmetry, complexity, and exact arithmetic}
\label{sec:rand-implementation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:implementation}
The mechanism \(\mathcal M\) is anonymous in utilities and
symmetric across goods. For \(n\) submitted reports, it uses
\(O(n^2\log n+n^2m)\) arithmetic operations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The priority order \(\omega\) is uniformly random on the
participating identities and independent of reports, so
relabelling bidders preserves the distribution of outcomes. All
other steps depend on goods only through cardinalities,
intersections, and independent identical survival probabilities,
giving symmetry across goods.

Only a single scale is executed. One greedy run takes
\(O(n\log n+nm)\) operations with explicit set representations,
and computing \(\kappa_i\) for all \(i\in G\) by deletion runs
takes \(O(n^2\log n+n^2m)\) operations.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact finite-precision arithmetic.}
When \(\xi\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\), the realized
payment~\eqref{eq:actual-payment} is continuous whenever
\(\xi C>\kappa_i\). Unlike conditional expected-payment
implementations (which sacrifice universal truthfulness),
Proposition~\ref{prop:reserve} and the discussion following it
allow an exact rational implementation that \emph{preserves
universal truthfulness}: simply replace
\(\xi\sim\operatorname{Unif}[0,1]\) by the discrete uniform
reserve \(\xi_M\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{1/M,2/M,\ldots,1\}\) with
\(M=m^2\). Because this discrete uniform law
satisfies~\eqref{eq:R}, Corollary~\ref{cor:mixture} ensures that
all incentive guarantees---including
universal truthfulness, ex-post IR, NPT, and FNP in
expectation---hold verbatim.

The welfare loss from discretization is negligible. At scale
\(\infty\), the highest bidder still satisfies \(V\ge C\ge\xi_M C\)
for every realization of \(\xi_M\), so \(\Phi_\infty^{(M)}\ge V\).
At any scale \(k\in\mathcal K\) and fixed \(\omega\), each
\(j\in G_k\) clears \(\xi_M C_k\) with probability
\(\lfloor M\min\{1,w_j/C_k\}\rfloor/M\ge w_j/V-1/M\) (and
probability \(1\) if \(C_k=0\)). Since \(|G_k|\le m\) and
\(w_j\le V\), multiplying by \(w_j\) and summing gives
\[
\sum_{j\in G_k}w_j^2
\le V\varphi_{k,\omega}^{(M)}+\frac{m}{M}V^2
=V\varphi_{k,\omega}^{(M)}+\frac{V^2}{m}.
\]
Repeating the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:welfare} with this
additive term inside the square root replaces
\(V\sum_k\Phi_k\) in~\eqref{eq:opt} by
\(V\sum_k\Phi_k^{(M)}+|\mathcal K|V^2/m\le|\mathcal K|\Phi^2(1+4/m)\),
yielding an approximation ratio of at most
\[
4\sqrt{2(m+4)\ln(em)\,|\mathcal K|}+\frac8e
=O\bigl(\sqrt m\log(m+1)\bigr).
\]
For rational reports, \(q_k(S)\) has denominator \((2k)^{|S|}\)
and \(\xi_M\) has denominator \(m^2\), so all weights,
thresholds, and payments are exact rationals of polynomial bit
length, and sampling \(\xi_M\) and \(\widetilde M\) requires only
elementary finite-precision draws.

\paragraph{Scope and extensions.}
The false-name guarantee is in expectation, with a physical bidder
liable for the payments of all its aliases. Thinning does not
impose independence of irrelevant goods, since adding a privately
desired good alters a bundle's survival probability. Finally, the
reserve-greedy analysis of Section~\ref{sec:rand-reserve} applies
without change if \(C(T)\) is computed separately on each
connected component of the conflict graph rather than globally;
this preserves universal truthfulness and FNP in expectation while
recovering full efficiency on disjoint single-item markets, though
the worst-case ratio over all profiles is unchanged.
